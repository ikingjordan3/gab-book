# Pharmacogenomics

Most of this book has asked which genomes get sick. This chapter asks a different question: given the
same diagnosis and the same drug, why do patients respond differently? **Pharmacogenomics (PGx)** is the
study of how a person's DNA affects their response to drugs, and it is one of the most direct routes from
genetic variation to clinical care — the genotype changes a decision taken for a patient today, rather
than a risk estimate for the future.

The first lecture, *Pharmacogenomics*, defines the field and works through two clinical examples end to
end: one where genotype changes the **dose** (efavirenz), and one where genotype decides whether a drug is
**prescribed at all** (ivacaftor). The second lecture, *Pharmacogenomic diversity*, adds the population
dimension. Pharmacogenomic variants are common, and common variants differ in frequency among populations,
so matching drugs to people becomes a public health question as well as a clinical one. That lecture
follows our own lab's work on two Colombian populations.

Work through the modules in order. Each one ends with a short self-check quiz. The chapter finishes with a
lab, {doc}`lab-pharmacogenomics`, where you translate star alleles into metabolizer phenotypes, apply a CPIC
guideline, and compare pharmacogenomic allele frequencies between populations.
